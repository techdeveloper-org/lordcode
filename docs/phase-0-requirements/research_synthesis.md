## 3. Progressive Summary — Step 3: Section Distillation, Grading, and Gap Classification

### 3.1 Q1 — OpenAI OAuth third-party eligibility

**Finding:** Unconfirmed. Current OpenAI documentation scopes the OAuth flow to OpenAI's own products by omission, not by explicit inclusion or exclusion. No source found states third-party eligibility either way.

**GRADE: VERY LOW.** Reasoning, applying the protocol's own §9 rule literally ("label HIGH only if a single explicit on-domain PRIMARY statement is found; otherwise VERY LOW/gap"): no explicit statement was found, so the rule's fallback applies directly. Independently of that rule, the same conclusion follows from first principles: the one PRIMARY source found (`learn.chatgpt.com/docs/auth`) supports the finding only by omission — an inherently weaker evidentiary pattern than an explicit statement, since silence is consistent with multiple underlying truths (undecided policy, policy that exists elsewhere and wasn't surfaced, or an oversight in the docs). The one corroborating source (TechCrunch, 2025-05-27) carries a decay weight of w ≈ e^(-1.39×1.25) ≈ 0.19 under source-evaluation-credibility-core's M5 model — roughly 81% credibility decay — and was used only for exploratory context, never as evidentiary support for a current-state claim, so it cannot lift the grade.

**Gap classification:** This is a mixed case and the two components must not be collapsed into one label.
- The core question — "does OpenAI's own documentation explicitly address third-party OAuth eligibility" — is an **Empirical gap**: the evidence map cell is empty. The live, current-state OpenAI page was read in full and contains no statement on the question in either direction. Nothing was missed due to access failure; there is simply nothing on that page to find.
- The dead link (`platform.openai.com/docs/guides/authentication` → 301 → 404) is a distinct, narrower **Reporting gap**: an authentication guide of some kind structurally should exist at OpenAI Platform (it is referenced/redirected-to by the URL structure), but the specific content is currently inaccessible via the path checked, not confirmed to say nothing on the topic. This sub-gap is a candidate for closure by a different navigation path (e.g., the platform docs index), not by assuming the redirect confirms absence.

**Must NOT be read as:** "OpenAI probably restricts this like Anthropic does" (an inference the protocol explicitly prohibits) or "OpenAI has no OAuth for third parties" (absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence).

**No blended verdict is given** — reporting "Gemini Go SDK: TRL 9" as one number would violate the protocol's explicit per-feature grading requirement and would also misrepresent that three structurally distinct API surfaces were checked independently.

### 3.3 Q3 — Specialist-agent corpus prior art

**Finding:** Non-refuting. Of four candidates surfaced by search, two were independently fetched and confirmed to fail the §3.2 bar (community-contributed, user-installed marketplaces, not vendor-bundled corpora). Two more — the two that most resemble LordCode's own claimed scale ("270 expert agents," "345 packages / 51 personas") — could not be fetched (HTTP 413 ×2, HTTP 403 ×2) and remain completely unresolved.

**GRADE: VERY LOW — capped per the protocol's binding constraint (§9 of `research_protocol.md`: "a non-refuting finding on Q3 is graded LOW or VERY LOW, never higher").** Independently of that cap, VERY LOW (rather than LOW) is the more defensible reading on the merits: the protocol's saturation criterion (§8 — "two consecutive fetched candidates failing the bar") was technically met, but only because the two candidates that were actually fetchable happened to be the weaker-looking ones; the two strongest-looking leads were never resolved for reasons of access failure, not evidentiary weakness. A finding whose saturation was satisfied by the candidates that happened to load, while the candidates most likely to refute it sit unexamined, warrants the lower of the two permitted grades.

**Gap classification — this is the one that must not be conflated with Q1's, and must not be conflated within itself:**
- AgentsRoom and the TechTimes-reported claude-skills library are a **Reporting gap**, not an Empirical gap. Both were located by a search engine (i.e., something is indexed and discoverable at those URLs / around that repository), and both fetch attempts failed with access-layer HTTP errors (413 Payload Too Large, 403 Forbidden) rather than returning empty or "not found" responses. The evidence-map cell for these two candidates is not empty — it is populated but currently unreadable. This is the taxonomy's textbook Reporting-gap pattern ("exists but is inaccessible or unconfirmed"), distinct from an Empirical gap ("nothing exists on this population/intervention").
- The two resolved candidates (`agency-agents`, `mcpservers.org/agent-skills`) are **not gaps at all** — they are closed, PRIMARY-confirmed findings (community marketplace pattern, not vendor-bundled corpus) and should not be counted toward the "unresolved" total.

**Must NOT be read as:** "no comparable prior art exists" or "LordCode's corpus approach is confirmed novel." The taxonomy classification above exists specifically to make clear that the two live unresolved leads are a Reporting gap (content believed to exist, currently unreachable), which is a categorically different, and much less reassuring, situation than an Empirical gap (a swept landscape genuinely turning up nothing). Treating this VERY-LOW, Reporting-gap-laden finding as though it were a clean empirical absence would be exactly the "absence of evidence as evidence of absence" anti-pattern both research-methodology-core and this agent's own operating rules prohibit.

### 3.4 Contradiction, reported explicitly (Operating Rule #4)

The tech-scout report (`tech_scout_report.md` §2.3) named the Gemini Go SDK's feature parity its **"largest gap in this dispatch"** and labeled all three sub-features **UNVERIFIED**, based on two failed PRIMARY-fetch attempts (repo root, raw README). The retrieval pass in `research_findings.md` **reverses this**: all three sub-features are now CONFIRMED at GRADE: HIGH (§3.2 above), using two artifacts (godoc, CHANGELOG) the earlier pass never tried.

**What changed:** Not the underlying facts — the SDK's feature surface did not newly appear between the two passes; both CHANGELOG entries substantiating streaming and caching predate the tech-scout report's own 2026-09-04 date by months. What changed is **fetch targeting**. The earlier pass checked the repo root and README and, finding neither conclusive, correctly declined to guess — it did not report "absent," it reported "unverified," which was the honest and correct call given what it had checked. The later pass, following the earlier report's own Recommended Follow-up #4 verbatim, checked godoc and CHANGELOG instead and found direct confirmation immediately.

**Which source supersedes which:** `research_findings.md`'s Q2 section supersedes `tech_scout_report.md` §2.3 for the specific claim "does the current Gemini Go SDK expose streaming/tools/caching." The earlier report's UNVERIFIED label should now be read as historical (accurate as of what was checked, at that time) rather than as a standing characterization of the SDK.

**Implication for the earlier report's other unverified/low-tier claims:** This episode demonstrates that at least one of the tech-scout report's "unverified" labels reflected a **fetch-targeting limitation of that pass**, not a true evidentiary absence or an unfavorable underlying fact. That is informative but must be applied cautiously — it is a single data point, not a general license to assume the report's other SEARCH-SYNTHESIS or SECONDARY-tier claims (e.g., Anthropic's third-party-OAuth-ban policy language, sourced one step removed from Anthropic's own policy page; Google One / Gemini Advanced's exclusion of API access, sourced from four converging but unfetched third-party summaries; the Flash-only free-tier blog claim, explicitly flagged UNVERIFIED by the tech-scout report itself) will resolve equally favorably once directly fetched. The correct inference is procedural, not substantive: **the tech-scout report's own Recommended Follow-ups list (§6 of that report) should be treated as high-value, not merely optional** — item #4 on that list is exactly what closed Q2 here, and items #1–#3 and #5 remain open with the same structural profile (a plausible primary source named but not yet fetched) that item #4 had before this pass closed it.
